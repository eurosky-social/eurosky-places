Places you can build on

A place lookup for check-ins, photo locations and maps, run in Europe, for anyone to use.

Many apps need to show geographic locations. A few American companies are presently the main suppliers of this data, shared through API keys and charging usage fees. They also gain access to user data.

places.eurosky.tech offers open geographic data. We use Foursquare Open Source Places, Overture Maps and OpenStreetMap, with about 112 million places worldwide, refreshed monthly. There is no key, no account and no cookie, and no logging of people’s locations. The code is open source.

    Live: what you type goes to the API as you type, from the centre of the city you pick, never your own position. Nothing is logged. Pick a result to see its full record.

    suggestions live

    $POST https://places.eurosky.tech/v1/suggestions

    What someone at a position could attach to a check-in or a photo: the places nearby, the street, and every area around them from the neighbourhood to the country. The app shows the list, the person picks, and the app publishes only the level they chose. The position goes in the body, never in the URL; the answer is not cached.

    curl -s https://places.eurosky.tech/v1/suggestions \
  -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"lat": 52.4867, "lng": 13.4245, "limit": 2}'
    fieldrequiredmeaning
    lat, lngyesWGS84 degrees
    radius50–2000, default 500metres to look for places
    limit1–100, default 30places to return

    • places: one entry per real place, ranked. origin is its ID in the most stable source, sameAs the same place in the other sources; group is a coarse kind (food and drink, sights, transit, …).
    • checkins: how often people pinned the place in posts published on the network (place pins), in total and in the last 30 days; popular places rank higher.
    • street: the nearest street, with a point in its middle (not the person's position), or null.
    • areas: the areas around the position, smallest first, each with a point inside it. Publish that point, not the person's, when they pick an area.
    • cityState: true marks cities that Overture files as regions (Berlin, Hamburg, Vienna, Prague, Seoul, …): treat that area as the city and the city-level areas inside it as its districts.

    search live

    $POST https://places.eurosky.tech/v1/search

    Type-ahead for a place picker: what the person is typing and, if the app has it, where they are, both in the body. Notable places and every area match worldwide in all their names and languages ("Brandenburg Gate", "Berlino"); with a position, everything within two kilometres matches too. Send it after about 150 ms without typing, as the panel above does.

    curl -s https://places.eurosky.tech/v1/search \
  -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"q": "Brandenb", "lat": 52.4867, "lng": 13.4245, "limit": 3}'
    fieldrequiredmeaning
    qyes, 1–100 charactersfewer than two letters or digits give no results
    lat, lngno, togetherwithout them only notable places and areas are searched
    limit1–30, default 10

    Each result has kind (place or area), origin, sameAs, name, how it matched (start, word, other_name, approximate), a category or area type, lat, lng and, with a position, distance in metres.

    squares live

    $GET https://places.eurosky.tech/v1/squares/{lat},{lng}

    For apps that would rather not send a precise position at all: everything in one square of 0.01° × 0.01° (about 1.1 × 0.7 km in Berlin), which the app then filters and ranks on the device. Name the square by its south-west corner, rounding the position down to two decimals. Everyone in a square asks for the same URL, so answers are cacheable for an hour and can sit behind any CDN.

    curl -s https://places.eurosky.tech/v1/squares/52.48,13.42
    • More than two decimals, other spellings of a square and any query parameter except page are refused (400 TooPrecise), so a careless client cannot leak its position in the URL.
    • places as in suggestions without distance, 500 per page, most important first; next links the following page.
    • areas on the first page: every area overlapping the square, with whole: true when it covers all of it and otherwise its outline inside the square, so the app can tell which areas its position is in.

    getPlace live

    $GET https://places.eurosky.tech/xrpc/org.atgeo.getPlace?uri={urn}

    One place or area by its ID, answered as an org.atgeo.place record. This is the lookup of the ATGeo gazetteer, the place-data effort of the AT Protocol community, so ATGeo clients can use this service as a gazetteer. IDs are ATGeo URNs, urn:atgeo:{dataset}:{collection}:{id}, the same origin and sameAs values every other endpoint returns.

    records draft

    Once someone has picked a place, the app writes it next to the post. We propose one small record for check-ins and for where photos and videos were taken, social.eurosky.place.pin: the place's URN plus the part of the place readers need to show it, put it on a map and group posts by it, at the precision the author chose, never the device's position. It is a draft for the ATGeo working group, and its name can still change.

    data

    Three open sources, loaded worldwide once a month, after Overture and Foursquare publish their releases. The same place in two sources is shown once, with the others in sameAs; records that only one weak source vouches for are kept out of suggestions.

    sourcelicencecredit when you show the data
    Foursquare Open Source PlacesApache 2.0Foursquare
    Overture MapsCDLA Permissive 2.0; ODbL for parts from OpenStreetMapOverture Maps Foundation
    OpenStreetMapODbL 1.0© OpenStreetMap contributors

    A month's load keeps the IDs each source uses; Foursquare and Overture IDs are the most stable, OpenStreetMap's can change when someone redraws a place, which is why every place carries sameAs.

    privacy

    • Positions travel in request bodies, never in URLs, and nothing that carries one is logged: the server keeps no access log, and its error log leaves request details out.
    • No key, no account and no cookie: requests carry nothing that says who sent them. To apply the limits, the server counts requests per address in memory for a minute, then forgets the address; it is never logged or stored.
    • Apps that would rather not send a precise position use squares, cacheable answers for a whole square kilometre.
    • The service runs on a dedicated server of ours in a Hetzner data centre in Germany. These pages load their fonts and scripts from it too; the demo map adds map tiles from OpenFreeMap (no cookies), avatars from Bluesky's image server and posts from Eurosky's public API.

    fair use

    The service is free and needs no key. Help keep it that way: wait about 150 ms after the last keystroke before searching, cache squares, and ask for one area of the map at a time. If you expect more than a few requests a second, or want to build something on it, write to team@eurosky.tech first. There is no service-level agreement yet.

    limit per client addressrequests a minute
    suggestions300
    search, squares, getPlace600 each

    Past a limit the answer is 429 {"error": "RateLimited"} with Retry-After; when the service is too busy to answer in time, 503 {"error": "Busy"} with Retry-After: 5. Both are safe to retry after that wait. The limits count each phone or browser on its own, so an app's users do not share one budget.

    The public endpoints answer requests from web pages on any domain (CORS).
